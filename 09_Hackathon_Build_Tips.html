Give CoCo the context first

Two minutes here saves twenty later.

CoCo reads AGENTS.md from your project root at the start of every session. Put your project’s shape in it once and you stop re-explaining yourself in every prompt.

# Project: Pipeline Risk Agent

## What we're building
A Cortex Agent that shows a sales manager which deals are
slipping, and why, using call transcripts as evidence.

## Data
SUMMIT_GEAR_CO.SALES.DEALS                 one row per deal
SUMMIT_GEAR_CO.SALES.SALES_CONVERSATIONS   call transcripts
Warehouse: SUMMIT_GEAR_WH

## Conventions
- Don't drop or recreate objects I already built
- Explain the approach before writing SQL

Ask CoCo to write it for you — “create an AGENTS.md describing this project” — then correct it. Faster than typing it yourself.

Seven things worth knowing

The difference between fighting CoCo and flying with it.

1. Describe the outcome, not the steps

You don’t need to know the SQL. Say what you want to be true at the end.

“I want a manager to open this and immediately see which three deals need attention this week, and what was said on the calls that makes them risky.”

2. Ask for the approach before the code

Have CoCo tell you how it intends to solve it first. Correcting a plan takes seconds; correcting a built thing takes minutes you don’t have.

“Before you build anything, tell me how you’d approach this and what you’d need from me.”

3. Paste the whole error

Don’t summarise it, don’t retype it. Give CoCo the complete message including any request or session ID. It reads detail you’d skip over.

4. One change at a time

Make a change, check it works, then move on. A single big request that half-works is harder to unpick than four small ones that each landed.

5. Ask it to find the holes

Your team has a sceptic. Give them a power tool.

“What assumptions did you just make? What would break this? What would a sceptical sales manager immediately distrust about these numbers?”

6. Say what you don’t want

Constraints are as useful as instructions. Keep it simple, don’t touch that table, no new dependencies, plain SQL only — say so and CoCo will hold the line.

7. Tell it the business context

You brought a client scenario with you. Give CoCo that, not just the technical task — it makes better choices about what to surface and what to ignore when it knows who’s looking and what decision they’re making.

If you get stuck

Everyone does. Here’s the fastest way out.

Restate the goal from scratch

Say the whole thing again, plainly, as if starting fresh. Half the time the fix is that CoCo is solving the problem you described three prompts ago.

Ask CoCo what it would do differently

“This isn’t working. What would you change about the approach?” It will often propose something better than the path you put it on.

Cut to one thing that works

One finished, demonstrable thing beats three half-built ones. If time is tight, pick the piece that best shows your idea and make that solid.

One last thing

The teams that get the most out of this hour aren’t the ones who know the most SQL. They’re the ones who ask for bigger things. Be ambitious with what you ask CoCo for — you’ll be surprised how often the answer is “yes, here it is”.
